# Sonnet 18, but it is a group chat

**by @theqmlguy**

> **Will:** ok hear me out. you. summer's day. compare?
>
> **Them:** ...go on
>
> **Will:** no wait actually you're better. summer is unhinged.
> wind trashes the flowers every May and the whole season is
> booked for like eleven weeks
>
> **Them:** you're calling summer short staffed
>
> **Will:** sometimes the sun is genuinely too much. other days it's
> hidden behind clouds and you get nothing. everything good looks
> worse eventually, either by bad luck or just time doing its thing
>
> **Them:** this is a lot
>
> **Will:** but here's the part I've been building to. your summer
> doesn't end. you don't get worse. death doesn't get to say it
> caught you, because you're in these lines now and the lines keep
> going
>
> **Them:** in the what
>
> **Will:** as long as anyone is alive to read this, this poem is
> alive, and so are you. that's the trick. I made you permanent by
> writing you down.
>
> **Them:** did you just commit me

---

**On the last line:** that joke is closer to the truth than it looks. Line 12 of the
original is *"When in eternal lines to time thou grow'st"*, and Shakespeare's whole
argument is that writing something down is what saves it from time. Which is not a
bad description of what you are doing every time you type `git commit`.
